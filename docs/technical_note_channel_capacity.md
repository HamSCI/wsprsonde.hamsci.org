# Channel Capacity of the WSPRSonde Network

**Status:** Technical Note 1, version 1.0
**Date:** 2026-09-11
**Editor:** Nathaniel A. Frissell, W2NAF (University of Scranton)
**Relates to:** [requirements](requirements_wsprsonde_management_system.md) Draft 0.8, R2.2,
R2.3, R2.4, §4.6 and open question 10.8

> **This is a working note, not a requirement.** It answers one question that came up while
> reviewing the frequency-coordination requirements: how many WSPRSondes can the world hold?
> Section 6 recommends a course of action and section 7 lists what would change in the
> requirements document if that recommendation is accepted. Neither has been applied.
>
> Every measured number here is regenerated by
> [`src/wsprsonde/window_occupancy.py`](../src/wsprsonde/window_occupancy.py). Run
> `PYTHONPATH=src python -m wsprsonde.window_occupancy` to re-measure.

## 1. Summary

The answer depends on which ceiling binds, and the four candidates are separated by more than
an order of magnitude.

| Ceiling | Units | What sets it |
|---|---|---|
| **Current allocation grid** | **15** | 10 Hz grid, 1450–1590 Hz (R2.2) |
| Full window, same grid | 20 | 1400–1600 Hz |
| Non-overlapping WSPR channels | 33 | 5.9 Hz occupied bandwidth in 200 Hz |
| What the window demonstrably carries | hundreds | measured in section 4 |

The binding ceiling is the first one; it is administrative, and the network reaches it during
the deployment now under way. The registry holds **two spare channels** once every unit
awaiting an assignment has one, against five further NSF-funded units yet to enter the
registry.

The recommendation in section 6 is to extend the grid across the full window and tighten its
step, which raises the ceiling to roughly 30 units at no cost to science cadence. The exact
figure depends on the guard band, which section 7 leaves to the frequency coordinator.

## 2. The administrative ceiling, which is the one that binds

R2.2 records the scheme in use: one offset per unit, applied to every band it transmits, on a
10 Hz grid from 1450 Hz upward. The window ends at 1600 Hz, and a 5.9 Hz signal centred at
1590 Hz still clears that edge, so the grid runs 1450 to 1590: **15 channels for the whole
network, worldwide**.

Eight bands buy no extra capacity, because a WSPRSonde-8 keys all of them on the same offset.
The channel is a global resource rather than a per-band one. The per-band override R2.2 asks
for is a correctness feature for the ZD7GWM case in §2.3 of the requirements, and it adds
capacity only where two units carry disjoint band sets. That case is real but rare: WB6CXC
runs two WS-8s at Occidental on 80–10 m and 160/6 m respectively, which keeps them clear of
§97.203(b)'s prohibition on two channels in one band from one station location.

Against 15 channels, the registry stands at:

```
Channel plan: 15 slots on a 10 Hz grid, 1450-1590 Hz
  in use on grid     5  [1450, 1470, 1480, 1500, 1550]
  off-grid           4  [1415, 1436, 1437, 1535]
  free              10  [1460, 1490, 1510, 1520, 1530, 1540, 1560, 1570, 1580, 1590]
  awaiting one       8  ['W4WB', 'K8TE', 'AC0G', 'WB4DH', 'K7ZOO', 'KB0KFH', 'WA4KFZ', 'W8GPS']
  spare              2
```

Ten free slots against eight units awaiting an assignment leaves two spare. CLAUDE.md records
ten NSF-funded units, of which five shipped in August 2026; the five that have not yet reached
the registry would put the grid three channels short. Retired units release their channels and are
excluded from the count above: AA8K, WO7I and W2RFC held 1425, 1445 and 1450, and WW0WWV now
holds 1450.

Two collisions are already on the books (§2.3 of the requirements). Running at two channels of
headroom is what produces them.

## 3. The waveform ceiling

WSPR-2 and FST4W-120 are both 4-GFSK at 1.46 Hz tone spacing and occupy 5.9 Hz. Franke,
Somerville and Taylor give the FST4W figures; Griffiths et al. state that "except for measured
spectral width and an SNR threshold of −31.4 dB WSPR-2 has the same parameters as FST4W-120".

| Submode | Tone spacing (Hz) | Occupied bandwidth (Hz) | SNR threshold (dB) | Channels in 200 Hz |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WSPR-2 | 1.46 | 5.9 | −31.4 | 33 |
| FST4W-120 | 1.46 | 5.9 | −32.8 | 33 |
| FST4W-300 | 0.56 | 2.2 | −36.8 | 90 |
| FST4W-900 | 0.18 | 0.72 | −41.7 | 277 |
| FST4W-1800 | 0.089 | 0.36 | −44.8 | 555 |

*Tone spacing, occupied bandwidth and SNR threshold are Table 1 of Griffiths et al. (2022),
which reproduces the Quick-Start Guide table and adds the WSPR-2 comparison. SNR thresholds
are in a 2,500 Hz bandwidth. The channel count is 200 divided by the occupied bandwidth,
rounded down, and it is an upper bound on any plan.*

The bottom three rows are not available to this network. Decoding requires Doppler spread
below the tone spacing, and best sensitivity requires it below one-eighth of the tone spacing.
On HF that constraint bites hard. Griffiths et al. find that on 20 m with FST4W-300 over a
two-hop path, "even with a quiet ionosphere, the spectral spread means that the decode
probability has already fallen to below 90%". They conclude that FST4W-900 "may only be of
practical value for one-hop ionospheric paths", and that FST4W-1800 "will only be of very
specialised use on non-ionospheric paths or on lower the HF bands".

A WSPRSonde exists to be decodable at intercontinental range, which is multi-hop by
definition. The honest waveform ceiling for this network is therefore the top row:
**33 channels per band**, and the same 33 network-wide under the one-offset-per-unit scheme.

FST4W-120 is worth having for other reasons. It is about 1.4 dB more sensitive than WSPR, and it
tolerates spreading better: with a 2 Hz Doppler shift it decodes at −24 dB where WSPR needs
−17 dB, and at 3 Hz of spectral spread WSPR fails at any SNR. It also reports spectral width,
which is a scientific measurement WSPR omits. None of that changes the channel count.

Occupancy is near-uniform, and the sonde region above 1450 Hz is marginally busier than the
bottom of the window. Casual WSPR randomises its transmit offset within the window on each
transmission, which is why one station appears in several bins and the column total exceeds
the 2,667 distinct transmitters in the table above. It is also why no allocation can be kept
clear by choosing a quiet spot: the traffic moves.

## 5. Why the sonde ceiling is so much lower than the WSPR population

Two kinds of collision behave differently, and coordination is worth its cost against only one
of them.

**Casual traffic is transient.** With a median 96 signals spread over 200 Hz on 20 m, a given
5.9 Hz channel expects about 2.8 co-channel signals per slot, so it is occupied in most slots.
A different set of stations occupies it each time, and they are not all mutually audible with
the sonde, so a sonde loses scattered decodes and keeps its long-run coverage. The 2.8 figure
is an upper bound for that reason.

**A second sonde is permanent.** It is GPS-disciplined, transmits in every slot, and overlaps
on all eight bands at once, so it degrades the same paths every slot indefinitely. §2.3 of the
requirements documents exactly this between ZD7GWM and N4RVE on 100 Hz.

Coordination buys avoidance of the permanent kind. It does not deliver a clean channel, and
R2.4 is right to require checking assignments against non-sonde activity. Draft 0.7 adopts
Gwyn Griffiths' co-channel dashboard on `wd2` for that check (§4.6), which is the right call.
This note predicts what it will show: on the crowded bands, almost every candidate channel is
busy in almost every slot.

This also disposes of spatial reuse as a deliberate lever. It demonstrably works, and the
ZD7GWM and N4RVE pair is the proof, since both are decoded on five common bands while sitting
within 0.4 Hz of each other on every one of them (§2.3 of the requirements). A reference
transmitter whose reliability depends on nobody in the overlap region listening is not a
controlled transmitter, so the project is right to treat that pair as a fault.

## 6. Recommendation

In priority order:

1. **Extend the grid to the full window and tighten the step.** A 6 Hz step yields 33 slots
   against today's 15, needs no hardware change, and costs nothing in observing cadence. A
   step of 6 Hz leaves almost no guard band, so the coordinator may prefer 7 or 8 Hz for a
   ceiling of 28 or 25; see section 7. Any of the three roughly doubles today's capacity, and
   this is the one to do now, before the remaining NSF units are configured.
2. **Adopt scheduled time-division if a second doubling is needed.** Rotating units through
   alternate slots doubles capacity again to roughly 60, and a sonde keyed in half the slots
   still returns 15 observations per hour per band. WSJT-X already implements the mechanism
   for FST4W as a round-robin control, so the concept is established practice. Whether the
   WS-8 firmware can schedule this way is a question for the manufacturer, and whether the
   science tolerates half cadence is the PI's call. It is listed second because it is the
   first lever that costs anything scientific.
3. **Add bands.** 60 m, 6 m, 630 m and 2200 m each open a further window, for the units that
   carry them. This helps least, because it adds capacity only for partial-band units.

Two options are worth naming and declining. Migrating to FST4W-300 for the extra channels
fails on multi-hop paths (section 3). Deliberate spatial reuse conflicts with the purpose of a
reference transmitter (section 5).

## 7. What would change in the requirements

Proposed, not applied. Each needs the frequency coordinator's agreement before it becomes a
requirement, since the grid is WB6CXC's to set.

- **R2.2** would record the grid as 6 Hz from 1400 Hz upward, keeping the four existing
  off-grid assignments as exceptions. The note's arithmetic assumes the reported offset is the
  signal centre; if it is the lowest tone, the top slot moves down by about 4 Hz and the count
  falls to 32.
- **R2.3** already requires a configurable guard band. What it lacks is a chosen value, and
  the value and the grid step have to be chosen together: a 6 Hz step with a 5.9 Hz signal
  leaves 0.1 Hz. Draft 0.8 called that no margin at all against a one-hertz measurement floor.
  **The floor is 0.1 Hz**, not one hertz, now that offsets are measured against
  `wsprdaemon.spots.frequency_mhz` (R3.3a), and KH2R and TI4JWC each hold a 0.3 Hz spread
  across their bands. The margin is still thin, because a guard band equal to the measurement
  floor tolerates no transmitter drift whatever, but the case for a 7 or 8 Hz step now rests on
  drift and on Doppler rather than on our inability to measure.
- **R2.4** would say what the adopted dashboard is for, given that every channel is busy. Its
  value is identifying a persistent co-channel occupant, and a channel it shows as clear at
  one receiver is not clear at another.
- **Open question 10.8** would gain the capacity finding, because "who moves off 100 Hz" and
  "the grid is nearly full" are the same problem.
- A new requirement may be needed for capacity reporting: the system should say how many
  channels remain, so the coordinator learns of exhaustion before an assignment fails.

## 8. Open questions

1. Is the assigned offset the signal centre or its lowest tone? This sets the top slot and
   changes the count by one.
2. Can WS-8 and BeaconBlaster firmware schedule transmissions into a subset of slots, as
   WSJT-X's round-robin control does for FST4W? **[open with WB6CXC]**
3. Does the science tolerate a half-cadence sonde if capacity demands it? **[PI decision]**
4. Should the grid reserve slots for the non-HamSCI units R2.7 anticipates, and how many?
5. Is 6 Hz with a 0.1 Hz guard band acceptable to the coordinator, or is a 7 or 8 Hz step with
   real margin the better trade against a lower ceiling of 28 or 25? The measurement floor no
   longer decides this (see §7); transmitter drift over temperature does, and nobody has
   measured that for the WS-8. **[open with WB6CXC]**
